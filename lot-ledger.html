Read this before you bid on anything

All 23 Amazon comps are carrier-unlocked. 27 of the 34 lots that look profitable are T-Mobile or AT&T locked — they cannot fetch an unlocked price until they're unlocked, and carrier liquidation stock is often not unlock-eligible. Every locked row below also shows what it earns if you have to sell it locked at a 20% discount. Six lots flip from profit to loss on that one assumption.

How these numbers were built

  • Selling price is the live Amazon Renewed price for the same model and capacity at the matching grade — Grade A→Excellent, B→Good, C→Acceptable — not the headline Excellent price. Grade discounts ran 4–21%, so this matters.
  • Cost per unit is the current bid ÷ units. Auctions are still open, so every one of these is a floor, not a price. Lots at 0–2 bids will move.
  • Modelled costs, all adjustable in auction-vs-amazon.csv: 8% Amazon referral, $6 fulfilment, $12 test/wipe/accessories/packaging, $6 inbound freight, 8% returns-and-defect allowance. Not modelled: buyer's premium, carrier unlock cost, storage, or capital held while inventory sells.
  • 56 of 93 lots have no comp — iPhone 17s, 13 Pro Max, 11 Pro/Pro Max, and every 512GB/1TB capacity aren't on the Amazon page-1 set you pulled. They're listed but unpriced, not judged unprofitable.
  • Velocity isn't modelled. A 457-unit lot means listing 457 units of one model into a market where you're the new seller. Profit per lot assumes they all sell near the current price; they won't if you dump them at once.
